# Chapter Zero
## Brand system · Version 1.0 · 20 October 2026

An online community for book exchanges, book discussions, recommendations, and critical thinking.

This system preserves the supplied identity and establishes proposed operating rules for future creative. The community purpose is confirmed by the founder; audience segments, messaging, layout specifications, and campaign examples below are strategic recommendations rather than claims about existing services. Examples are illustrative, not live offers.

## 01 · The brand idea

**Every story starts here.**

The existing tagline is our invitation. Our community idea is **a book is the beginning of a conversation.** Chapter Zero brings readers together to pass books on, discover another perspective, and think more deeply about what they read.

**Purpose:** Make reading a shared experience that opens access to books and different ways of thinking.

**Positioning:** For readers looking for their next book and people to explore it with, Chapter Zero is an online reading community connecting book exchange with thoughtful conversation. Its creative should show both the book and the human connection it makes possible.

**Brand promise:** A welcoming place to share books, explore ideas, and find your next perspective.

**Personality:** Curious, generous, thoughtful, warm, and grounded. Express enthusiasm through a specific observation or question. Give people room to disagree and room to begin.

**Core belief:** Reading becomes richer when we share what we read and question what we think.

## 02 · People and participation

These are participation needs, not demographic restrictions. Do not assume members share an age, location, budget, education level, or reading ability.

| Reader need | What we offer in communication | Desired action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I have books to pass on | A clear invitation to give a book another reader | Explore or offer an exchange |
| I want something worth reading | A recommendation with reasons, context, and suitable readers | Save a recommendation or join the conversation |
| I want to talk about a book | A focused prompt and a welcoming discussion | Share a view or ask a question |
| I want to think beyond the plot | An interpretation, evidence, and an alternative reading | Explain, question, or reconsider a reading |
| I am getting back into reading | Low pressure invitations and manageable entry points | Introduce yourself or choose a starting book |

**Participation journey:** Discover a book → find a point of connection → contribute at your own pace → return for another exchange or conversation. Build creative around one of these steps at a time.

Never imply a working exchange marketplace, delivery service, verification system, recommendation algorithm, or moderated event exists unless it actually does. Platform capabilities, exchange logistics, geography, eligibility, and fees must be confirmed before public promises are made.

## 03 · Message architecture

**Master message:** Share a book. Open a conversation.

**Supporting description:** Chapter Zero is an online community for exchanging books, sharing recommendations, discussing what we read, and exploring ideas together.

| Pillar | Message | Evidence to include | Example invitation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Books in circulation | Your finished book can be someone’s next beginning | Actual title, edition, condition, and exchange terms when applicable | What book are you ready to pass on? |
| Conversations with depth | A different reading can change how you see a book | A specific question and relevant passage reference | What changed your mind about this character? |
| Recommendations with reasons | Tell us why it stayed with you | A clear reason, genre, mood, and reader fit | Recommend a book and tell us who might love it. |
| Thinking with openness | Look closely. Make room for another interpretation | A claim, textual evidence, and another possible reading | What supports your interpretation? |

**Short bio:** Books to exchange. Ideas to explore. An online community for readers. Every story starts here.

**About copy:** A book can introduce you to a new idea—and to someone who sees it differently. Chapter Zero brings readers together to exchange books, share recommendations, and talk thoughtfully about what they read. Come with a favourite, a question, or a different point of view.

Use the original tagline for identity signatures. Use campaign headlines to say something specific; do not attach multiple taglines to the same piece.

## 04 · Brand voice

Sound like a well-read friend who listens carefully and always leaves space at the table.

| Principle | Write this way | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcoming | You can join with a question, too. | Real readers already know this. |
| Curious | What made the ending work for you? | This is the only correct interpretation. |
| Thoughtful | The repeated image of the door suggests… | This book is deep. Trust us. |
| Generous | If you enjoyed its quiet pace, try… | Everyone needs to read this masterpiece. |
| Clear | Share the title, author, and condition. | Facilitate literary asset redistribution. |
| Grounded | Here is one reader’s perspective. | This book will change everyone’s life. |

**Voice stays consistent; tone changes with the situation.**

| Situation | Tone | Sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome | Warm and low pressure | Welcome to Chapter Zero. What have you been reading—or hoping to read? |
| Exchange invitation | Useful and friendly | Ready to pass this one on? Share the edition, condition, and your exchange preferences. |
| Recommendation | Personal and specific | Try this if you enjoy unreliable narrators and endings that leave room for debate. |
| Critical discussion | Curious and precise | Which detail supports that reading? Could it suggest something else? |
| Disagreement | Calm and fair | You read that scene differently. What in the text led you there? |
| Community correction | Direct and respectful | Please discuss the interpretation without making personal remarks about the reader. |
| Product error | Brief and helpful | Your post did not publish. Your draft is still here. Try again. Use only if draft retention is implemented. |

### Writing mechanics

- Write the name **Chapter Zero** in prose. The joined **ChapterZero** spelling belongs to the supplied wordmark and established handles only.
- Use sentence case for headings and buttons. Reserve spaced capitals for short section labels and the existing logo tagline.
- Address the reader as “you”; use “we” for the community or a clearly identified team. Do not manufacture member consensus.
- Prefer short paragraphs, active verbs, concrete book details, and one question at a time. Explain literary terms when they matter.
- Use contractions naturally. Avoid forced slang, excessive exclamation marks, and decorative emoji strings. Use at most one purposeful emoji in an informal caption; none in critical guidance or service messages.
- Italicize book titles where supported; include the author on first mention. Label paraphrases clearly and verify quotations against the edition used.
- Mark **Spoilers: [book title]** before revealing plot details. Put the warning before the disclosure in captions, thumbnails, and video.
- Give content notes when relevant, without sensational detail. Respect author names, pronouns, translations, and cultural context.
- Ask permission before reposting member content or identifiable photographs; credit contributors and photographers appropriately.
- Recommendations are perspectives. Explain who a book may suit; avoid declaring a taste or genre intellectually superior.

### Before → after

“Only true bookworms will get this.” → “What detail stood out to you?”

“10 books you MUST read to become smarter.” → “Three books that offer different ways to think about belonging.”

“Swap now!!! Don’t miss out.” → “A finished book. A new reader. What are you ready to pass on?”

“You missed the whole point.” → “I read that passage differently. Here is the detail that shaped my view.”

## 05 · Visual direction

**An open reading room with the energy of a new discovery.** Cream space, deep green structure, confident Sora typography, and a purposeful orange signal create the recognizable base. Book pages, conversation, and the movement of a book between readers supply the content.

| Quality | Visible expression |
| --- | --- |
| Welcoming | Warm cream surfaces, natural light, generous margins, conversational copy |
| Thoughtful | Strong typographic hierarchy, clear attribution, restrained decoration |
| Connected | Paired views, book handoffs, reader annotations, questions with room for replies |
| Lively | One orange focal point, a confident headline, an unexpected reading question |

Avoid luxury publishing clichés, distressed vintage paper, dark academia styling, neon gradients, fake handwritten notes, crowded sticker collages, and generic education clip art. The existing sprout is a sign of growing ideas; do not turn every creative into a gardening theme.

**Fixed anchors:** Supplied logo artwork, the six core colors, Sora as the intended Latin typeface, calm hierarchy, and an inviting voice.

**Flexible elements:** Image selection, cream or deep green dominant layouts, headline scale within the hierarchy, content pillar, and the choice of one supporting graphic motif. Flexibility should change the story while retaining the same reading experience.

## 06 · Logo system

The six source files are retained unchanged in `New folder`. They are supplied PNG canvases, not production vector masters. Artwork occupies only part of several canvases: measure clear space from visible artwork, not the file boundary. Do not mistake the built-in background for transparency.

| Asset | Intended use | Constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary Logo.png | Covers, opening slides, brand introductions | Stacked composition needs generous vertical space |
| Horizontal Logo — Light.png | Cream mastheads and landscape signatures | Preserve supplied proportions and tagline |
| Horizontal Logo — Dark.png | Deep green mastheads and end cards | Contains a distinct simplified mark and lighter greens; preserve the complete asset |
| Logo Mark.png | Brand identifiers where the name is already clear | Detailed book lines need adequate size |
| App Icon.png | Profile images and app identity previews | Existing rounded canvas; platform submission requirements need separate exports |
| Color Palette.png | Source reference | Specified hex values are the system authority; exported pixels may differ |

**Clear space:** Let X equal the visible orange zero’s stroke width. Keep at least 2X clear on all sides of the complete lockup. Use at least 1X around the standalone mark. No headlines, rules, or photographic subjects may enter this area.

**Proposed minimum sizes, measured across visible artwork:** Horizontal lockup with tagline 320 CSS px / 65 mm; stacked with tagline 240 CSS px / 50 mm; detailed standalone mark 64 CSS px / 15 mm; supplied simplified app artwork 32 CSS px for preview. Check actual-size output. These are conservative working thresholds, not validated master specifications. At smaller sizes use an approved responsive export; never shrink an unreadable tagline or manually erase it from a PNG.

Do not stretch, tilt, outline, add shadows, change the colors, redraw the type, separate the book and sprout, or use the orange zero as a replacement letter in body copy. Keep original artwork away from busy photographs. Use an intact cream or deep green panel behind it.

**Production work still needed:** Obtain editable vector masters; create transparent light/dark lockups, a single-color version, a small-size mark, and platform-specific icon exports. The existing dark mark intentionally differs from the detailed light mark; retain both as supplied until a vector family is formally resolved. Never auto-trace and describe it as an original master.

## 07 · Color system

The source palette specifies these values. Use sRGB hex values for digital production; request a printer-managed CMYK conversion and physical proof for print rather than treating an automatic conversion as authoritative.

| Color | Hex | RGB | Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Zero Orange | #EE6A1B | 238, 106, 27 | Energy, focal points, limited action accents |
| Deep Spine | #0F4A2E | 15, 74, 46 | Headlines, primary text, dark panels, reliable action fills |
| Page Green | #1F8A55 | 31, 138, 85 | Secondary graphic fields, supporting shapes |
| Sprout Leaf | #3DB873 | 61, 184, 115 | Highlights, progress fills, illustration accents |
| Paper Cream | #FFFDF8 | 255, 253, 248 | Main canvas and reading surfaces |
| Ink Moss | #3D5A4A | 61, 90, 74 | Body copy, captions, supporting text |

**Default balance:** Approximately 60% cream, 25% greens combined, 15% orange, following the supplied palette guide. Treat this as a composition target, not a pixel quota. Dark chapter dividers may use Deep Spine as the dominant field with cream text and one orange accent. Book-cover colors can sit outside the palette inside clearly bounded imagery; do not recolor covers to make them match.

**Accessibility rule:** Normal text needs at least 4.5:1 contrast; large text needs 3:1. Large means at least 24 CSS px regular or about 18.67 CSS px bold. Essential controls and graphic boundaries need 3:1 against adjacent colors. Contrast alone does not establish full accessibility. See the calculated pairings in `contrast-audit.csv`.

**Safe everyday pairs:** Deep Spine on cream; Ink Moss on cream; cream on Deep Spine. Deep Spine on Sprout Leaf is reserved for large text. Page Green on cream is reserved for large text, not body links. Orange on cream meets 3:1 but fails normal text contrast: reserve it for large text or decoration. Cream or white text on orange fails normal text contrast. Deep Spine on orange also fails normal text contrast. Therefore small orange action buttons use the added utility ink **#102A1C**, with a Deep Spine boundary on cream; the default primary action remains cream text on Deep Spine.

**Derived functional colors, not new identity colors:** Utility ink #102A1C for readable text on orange; border #74867B for necessary input boundaries on cream; error #A12D25 for errors with explanatory text and an icon. Do not add these colors to decorative brand graphics. Success uses Deep Spine text and a check icon; never rely on Sprout Leaf alone to communicate status. Disabled controls remain readable and must be programmatically disabled.

## 08 · Typography

**Use Sora throughout the Latin brand system.** The supplied palette explicitly names Sora ExtraBold for the logo and Sora Regular/SemiBold for text. Keep this family rather than introducing an unrelated display face. Use the supplied wordmark as artwork; typing the name in Sora does not recreate the logo.

| Role | Weight | Desktop size / line height | Mobile size / line height |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Campaign display | 800 | 64 / 70 px | 40 / 46 px |
| Page title | 700 | 48 / 56 px | 32 / 40 px |
| Section heading | 600 | 32 / 40 px | 26 / 34 px |
| Card heading | 600 | 24 / 32 px | 22 / 30 px |
| Lead | 400 | 22 / 34 px | 20 / 30 px |
| Body | 400 | 18 / 29 px | 16 / 26 px |
| Button / navigation | 600 | 16 / 24 px | 16 / 24 px |
| Caption | 400 | 14 / 21 px | 14 / 21 px |
| Eyebrow label | 600 | 12 / 18 px | 12 / 18 px |

Use tracking -0.025em for display, -0.015em for page titles, normal for body, and +0.10em for short uppercase labels. Keep body copy left aligned with a ragged right edge, 45–75 characters per line, ideally around 65. Center short invitations or the primary logo, not long paragraphs. Use 400/600 for most work; 800 is an accent, not the default weight for everything. Never fake bold or stretch text.

**Social scale:** On a 1080px-wide canvas start with a 76–96px headline, 36–44px explanatory copy, and 28–32px credit. Review at approximately 360px wide; essential copy must remain readable. Simplify the copy before reducing its size. For A4 print start with 28–36pt titles, 16–20pt section heads, 10.5–12pt body at 1.45–1.6 line height, and captions of at least 9pt; proof at actual size.

**Fallback:** Sora, Arial, sans-serif. When Sora is unavailable in email or office software, use Arial consistently and review wrapping. Do not mix a fallback headline with a visibly different body family unintentionally. Sora is available under the Open Font License; obtain the actual font files and retain the license when distributing them. No font files are bundled in this package. The visual guide uses Sora if installed and otherwise the declared fallback, so it is not a font proof.

**Other scripts:** This release specifies Latin text. For Arabic or another script, commission a compatible script-specific type and layout review before release; do not rely on accidental glyph fallback or mirror Latin letter spacing.

## 09 · Layout and graphic language

**Spacing scale:** 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96 pixels. Use 8px as the default rhythm; 4px for optical adjustments. Align headline, body, and action to one shared edge.

**Digital layout:** Maximum content width 1200px; 12 columns with 24px gutters on desktop; 4 columns with 16px gutters and 20px outer margins on mobile. Reading columns max out at 68ch. Use 64–96px between major sections on desktop and 40–56px on mobile. Let content reflow at 320px without horizontal scrolling.

**Graphic layouts:** Use one headline, one dominant book or image, one invitation, and a modest identity signature. Leave at least 25% of the composition visually quiet. Put detailed event terms or multiple book descriptions in subsequent frames rather than filling the cover.

**Shape language:** Flat page panels with 16px corners; 24px for large feature panels. Use 8px for controls. Book-cover images preserve their real proportions and corners. Use a thin Deep Spine rule to suggest a spine or divider. Use an open oval arc as an occasional supporting motif, never a substitute logo. Show an inset or cropped page panel as a transition between ideas. Choose one motif per composition.

**Graphic hierarchy:** Background → content or image → heading → action → signature. Avoid gradients, glass effects, dramatic shadows, and decorative outlines around every element. Use a subtle boundary or space to group content. Do not repeat the sprout icon as a wallpaper pattern.

**Icons:** A single rounded line style at 2px stroke on a 24px grid. Use recognizable book, exchange, conversation, bookmark, and search symbols with text labels where meaning is uncertain. Do not mix filled and outlined icon families. Brand mark artwork remains separate from interface icons.

## 10 · Photography and illustration

**Photograph real reading culture:** Books moving between hands, readers talking at eye level, annotations made with permission, everyday shelves, and comfortable shared spaces. Include different ages, backgrounds, and ways of reading without tokenistic staging. Show reading as an accessible everyday activity.

**Art direction:** Soft natural light, warm neutral whites, true-to-life greens, moderate contrast, and visible paper texture from actual objects. Crop close enough to reveal a human action. Leave usable space for copy. Avoid heavy orange filters, crushed dark tones, artificial lens flares, and staged stacks used only as intellectual status symbols.

**Book covers:** Preserve title, author, color, and aspect ratio. Attribute title and author in the caption. Use permitted cover imagery or member photos with appropriate permission. Never fabricate a quotation, review, author endorsement, member testimonial, or event photograph.

**Illustration:** Simple flat forms, generous cream negative space, rounded line work, and two greens plus one orange accent. Subjects should explain a community action: a book handoff, two interpretations, a reading path. Avoid introducing a competing mascot or a second logo-like symbol.

**Image accessibility:** Describe the meaningful action in alt text. Keep key text outside images where the format supports it. If an image contains essential copy, repeat that information in an accessible caption or surrounding text.

## 11 · Digital components and behavior

These are design specifications for future implementations, not evidence of a functioning platform.

| Component | Anatomy and styling | Behavior and states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary button | Deep Spine fill, cream 600 text, 8px radius, minimum 44px height | Hover utility ink; pressed utility ink; visible focus; descriptive verb label |
| Orange emphasis button | Orange fill, utility ink text, 1px Deep Spine border | Hover retains pair and adds underline; use at most once in a view |
| Secondary button | Cream fill, Deep Spine text and border | Hover underline; never indicate state with color alone |
| Text link | Deep Spine, underline, clear destination | Preserve underline on hover; distinct focus outline |
| Book card | Cover, title, author, reason or exchange details, one main action | Entire card must not nest competing links inside a single link |
| Discussion card | Book reference, spoiler label if needed, question, attribution | Use real counts only; let the topic lead over engagement metrics |
| Exchange detail | Edition, language, condition description, location scope, terms | Required fields depend on the actual exchange model; do not display private addresses publicly |
| Recommendation card | Title, author, genre/mood, reason, suitable reader | Distinguish member opinion from editorial recommendation |
| Form field | Persistent label, 1px utility border, 48px height, help text | Error names problem and remedy; preserve entered data when supported |
| Notice | Short heading, explanation, optional next step | Error uses icon plus text; success uses check plus text |

**Focus:** 3px Deep Spine outline, 3px offset on light surfaces; cream outline on dark surfaces. A consistent focus indicator must remain visible on every background. Do not remove browser focus without a replacement.

**Interaction:** Support keyboard use and meaningful reading order; pair labels with controls; provide descriptive accessible names; aim for 44×44px touch targets; do not conceal critical instructions in hover-only content. Support zoom and text enlargement. Provide reduced motion alternatives.

**Motion:** 160–240ms for simple state transitions; 300–450ms for editorial reveals. Use a subtle page-slide or fade rather than bouncing letters or simulated page-flipping loops. Respect reduced-motion preferences with immediate state changes. Never animate long reading passages.

## 12 · Creative recipes

### Exchange invitation · 1080 × 1350

Cream canvas, 72px margins, small “BOOK EXCHANGE” label, Deep Spine headline, one book image or handoff photograph, one orange detail, identity footer. Headline: **Finished your chapter? Pass it on.** Supporting line: “Share a book you are ready to exchange.” CTA: “Tell us the title and condition.” Use only an available participation route; do not invent a listing feature or exchange event. Explain edition, location scope, and exchange terms in the caption or detail page.

### Recommendation carousel · 1080 × 1350 per frame

Five-frame structure: a precise reading interest → book and author → why a reader recommends it → who may enjoy it, plus a content note where useful → a question. Cover headline: **For readers who enjoy an unresolved ending.** Keep one book per content frame, covers intact, opinions attributed, and final action “What would you add?” Avoid invented book titles presented as real recommendations.

### Discussion prompt · 1080 × 1080

Cream canvas, 64px safe margin, Deep Spine question, small cover or text reference, Page Green separator, optional orange marker. Headline: **Did the ending change how you read the beginning?** Add book title and author, spoiler status, and a single invitation to explain. Do not expose a plot twist in a spoiler-warning thumbnail.

### Critical reading carousel · 1080 × 1350

Five-frame structure: question → one interpretation → passage location and verified short evidence → an alternative reading → invitation to weigh both. Use labels “A reading”, “The evidence”, and “Another possibility”. Frame interpretation as a reasoned position, not authorial intent unless documented. Distinguish author, narrator, and character.

### Story / vertical video · 1080 × 1920

Start with a human question in the first two seconds. Show an actual book or reader action, then one reason or perspective, then one invitation. Use large captions and cream/Deep Spine contrast. Keep key text approximately 90px from side edges and 250px from top and bottom as a starting safe zone; check the target platform’s current overlay preview before export. End with a 2-second identity frame. Use audio with permission, provide captions, and avoid frantic transitions.

### Community announcement

Use Deep Spine background, cream heading, orange date marker, and a cream details panel. State what, who, date, time zone, place or link, access requirements, and the next step. Never write “Join tonight” without a specific date/time for a potentially reshared graphic. Use real details, not placeholder event claims.

### Email / newsletter

Maximum 640px content column, cream background, horizontal identity header, one lead story, up to three short supporting sections, and one main action. Use live text, meaningful links, alt text, and an Arial fallback. Subject example: “A book to pass on. A question to bring.” Check the logo at its final display size and avoid embedding an entire email as one image.

### Print / presentation / merchandise

Print: cream base, Deep Spine text, one orange focal point, clear reading order. Use printer-requested bleed (often 3mm), safe margins, effective 300ppi imagery where appropriate, and vector logos when available. Do not stretch the current PNGs for signage.

Presentations: 16:9 canvas, one message per slide, 48px minimum outer margins on a 1920×1080 working canvas, 40–56px slide title, 26–32px body, and source credits that remain readable. Use cream content slides and Deep Spine section dividers. Apply voice rules to speaker notes as well.

Merchandise: keep the brand signature clear and let the material provide texture. Use approved one-color masters for embroidery or one-color printing once obtained. Do not manually flatten the multicolor PNG into a production master.

## 13 · Community editorial standards

Our creative invites the behavior we want in the community: curiosity, evidence, generosity, and respect. These are proposed editorial principles; publish a separate moderation and exchange policy before implying enforceable platform guarantees.

- Challenge an idea with reasons; do not attack a reader’s intelligence, identity, or taste.
- Distinguish personal response, textual observation, interpretation, and factual claim.
- Credit authors, translators, photographers, and members. Obtain permissions when required.
- Discuss sensitive topics with context. Do not market harm or controversy as clickbait.
- Keep spoilers clearly signposted. Invite alternate views without inviting harassment.
- Publish exchange condition and terms clearly; do not imply safety checks or delivery guarantees that have not been established.

**Content planning:** Rotate across the four pillars. A useful starting cycle is one exchange invitation, one reasoned recommendation, one discussion, and one critical reading prompt. Adjust using meaningful replies, successful participation where measurable, and member feedback. Avoid rewarding volume or reading speed as a measure of someone’s value.

## 14 · Creative brief and AI handoff

Before creating a piece, record: audience need, content pillar, one message, evidence or source, format and dimensions, one desired action, actual participation destination, accessibility requirements, permissions, and spoiler status.

**Reusable instruction:** “Create for Chapter Zero, an online community for book exchanges, discussion, recommendations, and critical thinking. Express a warm, curious, thoughtful reading community. Use Paper Cream #FFFDF8, Deep Spine #0F4A2E, restrained Zero Orange #EE6A1B, and Sora typography. Keep one clear message, generous space, a real book or relevant human action, and one invitation. Preserve the supplied logo artwork. Use readable color pairs, credit sources, flag spoilers before disclosure, and never invent reviews, events, features, quotations, or community statistics. Deliver for [format], to help [reader need], around [verified content], with the action [available next step].”

AI-generated visuals must not fabricate legible book covers, actual member activity, endorsements, or author likenesses presented as documentary evidence. Add real verified title and author text in the layout tool. Use generated imagery only when it serves the brief and is clearly appropriate to its context.

## 15 · Review and governance

### Release checklist

- Does it clearly serve exchange, discussion, recommendation, or critical thinking?
- Is there one useful message and one available next action?
- Does the copy invite participation without judging reading taste or experience?
- Are book details, claims, quotations, and credits verified?
- Are spoilers and content notes positioned before the relevant material?
- Are logo proportions, clear space, and final-size legibility preserved?
- Do the palette, Sora hierarchy, and quiet space make this recognizable as Chapter Zero?
- Are text contrast, captions, alt text, keyboard access where applicable, and small-screen readability checked?
- Are images, member content, and production assets cleared for their intended use?
- Are dates, time zones, exchange terms, destinations, and platform capabilities accurate?

**Ownership:** Assign one brand steward to maintain this source document and approve new identity assets. Routine content can be produced using these rules without revisiting the identity. A changed logo, type family, core palette, or tagline requires a documented brand-system revision. Keep an archive and date every release.

**Naming:** `CZ_[pillar]_[format]_[topic]_[YYYY-MM-DD]_v01` with `_draft` or `_approved` status. Store editable source alongside exports, credits, and the completed brief. Separate campaign experiments from identity masters.

**Definition of ready:** A piece passes the checklist, has verified content and a working destination, and is checked at actual size on its intended channel. A consistent visual alone is insufficient if the invitation cannot be fulfilled.

**Remaining assets:** Editable vector logo family; transparent and one-color exports; tested small-size icon; licensed font files; reusable native templates in the team’s chosen design tool; and actual product, exchange, and moderation policies. This package supplies specifications and visual examples, not those missing masters or a working community application.
